Prototype: 6v6 alien football, automated CPU vs CPU, in an isolated test scene.

## §1 Goal

A game of alien football that plays itself and is genuinely watchable. Success is: Sam can sit in the stands, watch a full 10-minute game with no input, and the scoreline and the plays both look like football happened.

Phase 2 (player subs in at QB) is NOT being built here, but the architecture must make it a drop-in. See §9.

**Explicitly out of scope for Phase 1:** stadium art, crowd, betting, concessions, coach NPC, dialogue, multiplayer, player input of any kind.

## §2 Build order

Do not skip step 1. If step 1 doesn't read as football, stop and tell Sam before building anything else.

1. `[BUILD]` **Gray box.** 12 capsules on a flat plane, no stadium, no models, no UI. One formation, one play, man coverage, snap → throw → catch or incomplete → reset. Sam watches it. Go/no-go.
2. `[BUILD]` Full down and drive state machine, play table, running plays, tackling, turnovers.
3. `[BUILD]` Kickoff, quarters, clock, scoring, full game loop.
4. `[BUILD]` Debug tooling (§8). Build this before tuning, not after.
5. `[INTEGRATE]` Swap capsules for the existing alien models. Sam places the stadium asset around the field last.

## §3 Scene setup

`[AUTHOR]` New scene: `Proto_Football.unity`. Nothing from the main game loads into it.

**Field root.** A single empty GameObject `FieldRoot`. Every position in the sim is expressed in `FieldRoot` local space, never world space. This is non-negotiable — see §10.

`[OPEN]` **Field dimensions.** Starting proposal, Sam to confirm: 55 yards of playing field plus two 10-yard end zones (75 total), 30 yards wide. A full 100-yard NFL field is far too empty with 6 players a side. First down = 10 yards.

`[OPEN]` **Unity units per yard.** Propose 1 unit = 1 yard for readable math. Confirm this doesn't fight the existing character controller's scale and movement speeds.

## §4 Architecture

The core idea: **every player slot runs a swappable brain.** Nothing else in the sim knows or cares whether a brain is a CPU or a human.

```
FootballMatch          - owns the game: quarters, clock, score, possession
  └ DriveController    - owns a drive: down, distance, ball spot, play selection
      └ PlayInstance   - owns one snap: assigns brains, runs the play, reports result
          └ PlayerSlot × 12
              └ IPlayerBrain  (CPU implementations in Phase 1)
```

`[BUILD]` **`IPlayerBrain`** — one interface, one method per tick, receives a read-only snapshot of the play state (ball position and state, every player's position and team, time since snap, the slot's own assignment) and outputs a desired movement vector and optional action (throw, snap, dive). Brains never mutate the world directly. The slot applies the output.

**Phase 1 brain implementations:**

- `[BUILD]` `OLBrain` — engage the nearest unblocked rusher, mirror him, apply a shove that degrades over time (§6).
- `[BUILD]` `WRBrain` — run the assigned route as a waypoint list, then improvise toward open space. When the ball is airborne and targeted at this slot, move to the intercept point.
- `[BUILD]` `QBBrain_CPU` — drop back, scan, throw or scramble (§7).
- `[BUILD]` `DLBrain` — pursue the QB, resolve against a blocking OL, pursue the ballcarrier once the ball is loose or the QB crosses the line.
- `[BUILD]` `DBBrain` — man coverage on the assigned WR with a leash distance and a reaction delay. Break on the ball when it's airborne and the intercept point is within reach.
- `[BUILD]` `LBBrain` — one defender is a spy: shadows the QB laterally, rushes if the QB holds too long, covers the short middle. `[OPEN]` Sam may prefer a fourth rusher instead. Default is the spy.
- `[BUILD]` `BallCarrierBrain` — overrides whatever brain the slot had the moment it possesses the ball. Runs toward the target end zone, steers away from the nearest pursuer, straight-arms nothing (no juke system in Phase 1).

`[BUILD]` **Team stat block** — each team gets a small set of 0–1 floats: `blocking`, `passRush`, `coverage`, `qbAccuracy`, `speed`. These feed the timers and error terms below. This is what makes two teams distinguishable and what betting odds will eventually read from. Keep it to five numbers.

## §5 Game flow

`[BUILD]` `FootballMatch` state machine:

```
CoinToss → Kickoff → DriveLoop → (score | turnover on downs) → Kickoff → ...
                                           ↓
                                    QuarterEnd ×4 → GameOver
```

**Clock.** `[OPEN]` Proposal: 2.5 minutes per quarter, running continuously including between snaps, stopping only for scores and the kickoff reset. At roughly 6 seconds of live play plus a 4-second reset, that's about 15 plays a quarter and 60 a game, landing the whole game near 10 minutes of real time. If the clock instead stops on incompletions and out of bounds, a 10-minute game becomes a 25-minute game. Sam confirms which he wants.

**Downs.** 4 downs, no punting. Failure to convert = turnover on downs at the spot. First down resets to 1st and 10.

**Scoring.** `[OPEN]` Proposal: touchdown = 7, no kicking game at all, no field goals, no safeties in Phase 1. Simplest thing that produces believable scorelines.

**Kickoff.** `[BUILD]` Fully automated in both phases. Ball is launched to a fixed target zone with small random spread. The receiving team's designated returner catches it (mesh touch, §6) and enters `BallCarrierBrain`. Play ends on tackle or touchback. Sam has confirmed the player will never return kicks.

## §6 Ball and contact

`[BUILD]` **Ball** — a physics rigidbody with a parabolic launch. No custom trajectory faking; use real physics so the arc preview in Phase 2 matches reality.

`[BUILD]` **Catching — mesh touch, Football Fusion style.** The ball has a trigger collider. The first player collider it touches while airborne takes possession. No catch probability roll, no animation gate. If nobody touches it, it hits the ground and the play is dead (incomplete) — except on a kickoff, where it stays live until recovered.

This single rule gives interceptions for free: if a defender's mesh is in the path, he catches it.

`[BUILD]` **Interception return.** On a defensive catch, possession flips immediately, the interceptor enters `BallCarrierBrain`, his teammates switch to pursuit-blocking (just run at the nearest opponent), and the former offense switches to pursuit. Play ends on tackle. This costs almost nothing because it reuses the ballcarrier and pursuit code the kickoff return already needs.

`[BUILD]` **Tackling** — sphere trigger on the ballcarrier. Any opposing player entering it ends the play at the contact point. No tackle-breaking, no gang tackles, no animation blending in Phase 1.

`[BUILD]` **Blocking / pocket** — do not simulate real pass protection. Each OL is paired with a rusher and applies a positional shove. The pocket has a lifetime rolled at the snap from `blocking` vs `passRush`, in the 2.0–4.0 second range. When it expires, one rusher breaks free and takes a direct line at the QB.

## §7 The CPU quarterback

This brain does the most work and is the one that will decide whether the games look smart or stupid. Tune it last, after the debug tools exist.

1. Snap. Drop back for a fixed drop time (~1.2s).
2. Scan receivers in the play's priority order. For each, compute an openness score: distance to nearest defender, plus how much of that separation will still exist by the time a throw arrives.
3. If any receiver's score clears a threshold, throw to him.
4. If the pocket collapses or the hold timer expires with nobody open, scramble — enter `BallCarrierBrain` and run.
5. `[OPEN]` Throwaways: does the CPU QB ever intentionally throw the ball away, or does he always scramble? Default is always scramble.

`[BUILD]` **Throw accuracy** — aim at a lead point ahead of the receiver, then add error scaled by `qbAccuracy` and by whether a rusher is within X units at release. Overthrows and behind-the-receiver misses come out of this naturally and will produce the occasional pick, which is what you want.

`[BUILD]` **Play table** — roughly 6 plays, each a set of route waypoint lists per WR plus a priority order. Weight selection by down and distance.

Starting set `[AUTHOR]`: slant, out, deep go, crossing route, WR jet sweep, QB draw. Sam can expand later; don't build a play editor.

## §8 Debug tooling

Build this before tuning. Without it the sim is untunable.

- `[BUILD]` Time scale control (0.25× to 4×) with a hotkey.
- `[BUILD]` On-screen play-by-play log — down, distance, play called, result, yards. This is what Sam reads to judge whether the game is sane.
- `[BUILD]` Free camera detached from any player.
- `[BUILD]` Force-a-specific-play dropdown, and a restart-drive button.
- `[BUILD]` End-of-game summary dump: final score, total plays, yards per play, interceptions, turnovers on downs, longest play.

## §9 Phase 2 hooks — design in, don't build

- The QB slot must accept a `PlayerQBBrain` with zero changes to `PlayInstance`, `DriveController` or any other brain.
- `PlayInstance` exposes an event when a play ends and its result, which the coach recap will later read.
- `FootballMatch` exposes possession state, so Phase 2 can lock the player out of the field when his team is on defense.
- Do not hardcode "the QB is a CPU" anywhere.

## §10 Integration constraints — read before writing any code

These are the two things that will force a painful rewrite if ignored.

1. `[INTEGRATE]` **Floating origin.** The main game shifts world origin constantly. Every position, target, waypoint and trajectory in this sim must be computed in `FieldRoot` local space and converted only at the last moment for rendering. If any brain caches a world-space position across frames, the sim will break the instant it ships into the real game.
2. `[INTEGRATE]` **Planet gravity.** On Cyclops, "down" points at the planet centre, not at `-Vector3.up`. Treat the field as locally flat: take up from `FieldRoot.up`, and express all movement and ball physics relative to that. Do not assume world up anywhere, including in the ball's launch math.

Neither of these matters in the prototype scene. Both matter the day it moves to Cyclops, and retrofitting them is much worse than respecting them now.

## §12 Phase 1 acceptance test

`[TEST]` Sam watches 5 complete games at 2× speed with the play-by-play log visible. Phase 1 passes when:

- No game hangs, loops forever, or ends in a state the match machine can't leave.
- Final scores land in a believable range. Games ending 0–0 or 63–3 mean the tuning is wrong.
- Yards per play sits in a sane band (roughly 3–8). Wildly higher means coverage is broken; near zero means the pocket collapses too fast.
- Interceptions happen but are not routine — a couple a game, not one a drive.
- Watching a drive is actually interesting without any commentary or UI to explain it.

Until all five hold, do not start Phase 2.
