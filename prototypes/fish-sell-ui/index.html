FISH SELL UI — FOUR DIRECTIONS

Click things — each one stages fish and totals live. Same data in all four: you're at Icey Twin with seven fish in the bag, three of them worth a trip. The number the market pays is always base × bucket × appetite, shown before you commit. The question each mockup answers differently: where does "what do they want" live, and how do you pick what to sell?

Ledger. Two columns, no modes. Your bag on the left, what this market pays on the right, always both visible. Tick fish to sell them; the right column highlights the matching rate so you can see why a fish is worth what it is. Least change from today, most information on screen. Risk: dense on a controller.

Counter. Physical metaphor: the market's wants are a strip of chips along the top, your fish are cards in the bag below, and clicking a card puts it on the counter. Whatever is on the counter is what gets sold. Reads at a glance, feels like handing fish over. Risk: the chip strip gets long on a main world with 14 listed species.

Phosphor receipt. Same family as the PHOSPHOR dialogue UI: a terminal printing a receipt. One table — species, weight, the rate this market pays, what you'd get — and the "wanted" list is just the rows at the bottom you don't have yet. Best on keyboard/controller (up/down/space), cheapest to build, fits the game's other screens. Risk: less warm; no fish pictures.

Focus. One fish at a time, big, with the price worked out in front of you — base, the bucket word, the appetite dots — and a strip of the rest along the bottom. Sell this one, or add it to a batch. The most "game" of the four and the most controller-friendly (left/right, A). Risk: slow if you have twenty fish.
